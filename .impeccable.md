## Design Context

### Users

The product is used by two distinct audiences: infrastructure administrators who operate the public FRPS control plane, and technical users who run a Client Panel on their own workstation or LAN host. Administrators need trustworthy global visibility and safe recovery workflows; users need a fast, low-friction way to authenticate to a chosen Server Panel, manage tunnels, and understand whether a change is merely reserved, pending, running, offline, or failed.

### Brand Personality

Calm, precise, operational. The UI should evoke confidence during incidents, make resource ownership and state obvious, and keep high-risk actions deliberate. Copy should be concise and concrete rather than playful or promotional.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use an industrial control-room direction influenced by mature infrastructure products: graphite surfaces, warm ivory text, restrained steel-blue structure, and amber/green/red state colors used only when they communicate meaning. Avoid pale purple, candy gradients, neon cyberpunk, generic SaaS card grids, and decorative glassmorphism. Prefer dense but breathable layouts, strong tabular information hierarchy, clear status rails, and typography that feels editorial and technical without defaulting to system monospace.

### Design Principles

1. State before decoration: every status, lease, revision, and operation should be legible at a glance.
2. Calm under pressure: use a neutral dark foundation and reserve saturated color for actionable states and warnings.
3. One control surface, two products: keep Server admin and Client user workflows visually related but structurally separate.
4. Evidence over implication: show desired versus observed state, revision/version, timestamps, and the next safe action.
5. Respectful friction: make destructive operations explicit, reversible where possible, and impossible to trigger accidentally.
